\section{Theoretical analysis}

\newtheorem{adaptationlemma}[theorem]{Lemma candidate}
\newtheorem{adaptationproposition}[theorem]{Proposition candidate}
\newtheorem{transportproposition}[theorem]{Proposition}

\subsection{Minimal vector linear specialization}

We first analyze one teacher-induced update and then transport its effect
through subsequent learning.
The results have explicit algebraic proofs, but
remain preliminary with respect to the complete delayed-feedback system and
do not constitute novelty claims or sequential optimality guarantees.

Under squared loss, consider
\begin{align}
    Y &= (w^*)^\top X + \epsilon,
    &
    F_\theta(x) &= \theta^\top x,
    \\
    D(x) &= (w^*+b)^\top x + \nu,
    &
    e &= \theta-w^*,
    \\
    M &= \mathbb E[XX^\top] \succ 0,
    &
    \alpha &= x^\top e,
    \qquad
    \beta = x^\top b .
\end{align}
The matrix $M$ is the second-moment matrix of the future input distribution
and need not be a centered covariance matrix.

The teacher noise satisfies, conditionally on the current input,
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb E[\nu\mid x]=0,
    \qquad
    \mathbb E[\nu^2\mid x]=\sigma_D^2.
\end{equation}
For the current task-loss comparison, we also assume
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb E[\epsilon\mid x]=0,
    \qquad
    \mathbb E[\epsilon\nu\mid x]=0,
\end{equation}
with finite second moments. These are assumptions of the present
specialization rather than consequences of marginal noise moments. No
Gaussian distribution or full independence assumption is required.

Holding the parameters and the pre-query state fixed, the expected
immediate gain from replacing the cheap response by the teacher response is
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_{\mathrm{now}}(x)
    = \alpha^2-\beta^2-\sigma_D^2,
    \label{eq:immediate-gain}
\end{equation}
where the common irreducible task-noise contribution cancels.

The population excess-risk component of the cheap predictor is
\begin{equation}
    R(e)=e^\top M e.
    \label{eq:population-risk}
\end{equation}
This quantity is not the total system objective: it excludes routing costs,
future operational decisions, and irreducible task noise.

When the teacher is queried, its same operational response is used as an
immediate pseudo-target:
\begin{align}
    \theta^+
    &= \theta+\eta x\bigl(D(x)-\theta^\top x\bigr),
    \\
    e^+
    &= e-\eta x(\alpha-\beta)+\eta x\nu,
    \qquad \eta>0.
    \label{eq:error-update}
\end{align}

\begin{adaptationlemma}[Exact single-update adaptation value]
Under the conditional teacher-noise assumptions above,
\begin{align}
    \Delta_R(x)
    &:= R(e)-\mathbb E[R(e^+)\mid x]
    \notag\\
    &= 2\eta(\alpha-\beta)x^\top M e
    -\eta^2
    \bigl[(\alpha-\beta)^2+\sigma_D^2\bigr]
    x^\top Mx .
    \label{eq:exact-adaptation}
\end{align}
\end{adaptationlemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $d=\alpha-\beta$. From~\eqref{eq:error-update},
\begin{equation}
    e^+=e+\eta x(\nu-d).
\end{equation}
Expanding the quadratic risk gives
\begin{equation}
    R(e^+)
    =
    R(e)
    +2\eta(\nu-d)x^\top Me
    +\eta^2(\nu-d)^2x^\top Mx.
\end{equation}
Conditioning on $x$,
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb E[\nu-d\mid x]=-d,
    \qquad
    \mathbb E[(\nu-d)^2\mid x]=d^2+\sigma_D^2.
\end{equation}
Taking expectations and subtracting the result from $R(e)$ yields
\eqref{eq:exact-adaptation}.
\end{proof}

Equation~\eqref{eq:exact-adaptation} is the central algebraic result of the
minimal model. The two quantities
$\Delta_{\mathrm{now}}$ and $\Delta_R$ describe different effects of the
same routing action. The former measures the expected quality gain of the
current operational response, whereas the latter measures the expected
change in the future population risk of the cheap predictor induced by
training on that response.

Since
\begin{equation}
    \nabla R(e)=2Me,
    \qquad
    x^\top Me=\frac{1}{2}x^\top\nabla R(e),
\end{equation}
the first-order adaptation effect for small $\eta$ is governed by
\begin{equation}
    (\alpha-\beta)x^\top Me.
    \label{eq:geometric-factor}
\end{equation}
Thus, local teacher quality and training value are not equivalent: a teacher
response can improve the current prediction while inducing an update that
increases population risk, or conversely.

\subsection{Compatibility under isotropic risk geometry}

The first result identifies a setting in which immediate superiority and
positive adaptation value cannot conflict under a conservative update.

\begin{adaptationproposition}[Isotropic compatibility]
Suppose $M=cI$ with $c>0$ and define
\begin{equation}
    a:=\eta\|x\|^2.
\end{equation}
If
\begin{equation}
    0<a\leq1
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    \Delta_{\mathrm{now}}>0,
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_R>0.
\end{equation}
\end{adaptationproposition}

\begin{proof}
From $\Delta_{\mathrm{now}}>0$,
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_D^2<\alpha^2-\beta^2.
    \label{eq:teacher-better-condition}
\end{equation}
In particular, $|\alpha|>|\beta|$, which implies
\begin{equation}
    \alpha(\alpha-\beta)>0.
\end{equation}
Under $M=cI$, equation~\eqref{eq:exact-adaptation} becomes
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_R
    =
    c\eta
    \left[
        2\alpha(\alpha-\beta)
        -a\bigl\{(\alpha-\beta)^2+\sigma_D^2\bigr\}
    \right].
\end{equation}
Using~\eqref{eq:teacher-better-condition},
\begin{align}
    (\alpha-\beta)^2+\sigma_D^2
    &<
    (\alpha-\beta)^2+\alpha^2-\beta^2
    \notag\\
    &=
    2\alpha(\alpha-\beta).
\end{align}
Since $a>0$,
\begin{align}
    \Delta_R
    &>
    2c\eta\alpha(\alpha-\beta)(1-a).
    \label{eq:isotropic-bound}
\end{align}
For $0<a<1$, the right-hand side is strictly positive because
$\alpha(\alpha-\beta)>0$. For $a=1$, the strict inequality in
\eqref{eq:isotropic-bound} directly gives $\Delta_R>0$.
\end{proof}

The condition $0<a\leq1$ will be referred to as the conservative
no-overshoot regime. For a repeated fixed input, the noiseless residual
multiplier is $1-a$, whose magnitude is below one for $0<a<2$.
The interval $1<a<2$ is therefore locally stable in this restricted sense,
but permits overshoot. Such local stability does not imply a decrease in
population risk in the presence of noise, anisotropic risk geometry, or
changing inputs. The compatibility result above is therefore not extended
to the entire interval $0<a<2$.

\subsection{Geometric conflict under anisotropic risk}

The isotropic result does not extend universally to anisotropic
second-moment matrices.

\begin{theorem}[Anisotropic conflict; preliminary]
Let $M\succ0$ be symmetric and not proportional to the identity. Then there
exist $x$ and $e$, together with a noisy teacher unbiased with respect to
the conditional mean
\begin{equation}
    b=0,
    \qquad
    0<\sigma_D^2<\alpha^2,
\end{equation}
such that
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_{\mathrm{now}}>0
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    \Delta_R<0
\end{equation}
for every $\eta>0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Because $M$ is not proportional to the identity, there exists a nonzero
$x$ that is not an eigenvector of $M$. Hence $x$ and $Mx$ are not
collinear, and strict Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\begin{equation}
    (x^\top Mx)^2
    <
    \|x\|^2\|Mx\|^2.
\end{equation}
Since $x^\top Mx>0$, the interval
\begin{equation}
    \frac{x^\top Mx}{\|Mx\|^2}
    <
    k
    <
    \frac{\|x\|^2}{x^\top Mx}
    \label{eq:k-interval}
\end{equation}
is nonempty.

Choose any $k$ satisfying~\eqref{eq:k-interval} and set
\begin{equation}
    e=x-kMx.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{align}
    \alpha=x^\top e
    &=\|x\|^2-kx^\top Mx>0,
    \\
    x^\top Me
    &=x^\top Mx-k\|Mx\|^2<0.
\end{align}
Now take $b=0$, so that $\beta=0$, and choose
\begin{equation}
    0<\sigma_D^2<\alpha^2.
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:immediate-gain} then gives
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_{\mathrm{now}}
    =\alpha^2-\sigma_D^2>0.
\end{equation}
On the other hand,
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_R
    =
    2\eta\alpha x^\top Me
    -
    \eta^2(\alpha^2+\sigma_D^2)x^\top Mx.
\end{equation}
The first term is strictly negative because
$\alpha>0$ and $x^\top Me<0$, while the second is strictly negative because
$M\succ0$ and $\eta>0$. Therefore
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_R<0
\end{equation}
for every $\eta>0$.
\end{proof}

The theorem is an existence result. It does not state that every query under
anisotropic risk geometry exhibits a conflict. Rather, isotropy guarantees
compatibility in the conservative regime, whereas anisotropy permits
configurations in which a teacher that improves the current expected
prediction induces a population-risk-increasing update, even for
arbitrarily small step sizes.

The construction is algebraic and does not imply that the conflicting
inputs have positive probability under every distribution sharing the same
second-moment matrix $M$. Establishing distributional conditions under which
such conflict occurs with nonzero probability is outside the present
single-update analysis.

Neither harmful updates nor gradient misalignment by themselves are claimed
as novel contributions. Their relevance here is that they can alter the
value of an operational routing decision when the same costly response is
also used to adapt the cheap predictor.

\subsection{Transport under common subsequent learning}
\label{sec:transport}

We now extend the single-update analysis in time. The question is how
future learning transforms the benefit or harm caused by querying the
expensive predictor now. This is a minimal exactly analyzable model of
adaptation value, without a claim of new SGD theory or sequential optimality.

Condition on the current information, with $e_t=e$ and $x_t=x$ fixed;
all expectations in this subsection use this conditioning. Write
$\eta_D>0$ for the initial pseudo-update step (the $\eta$ of the preceding
single-update results), reserving $\eta$ for subsequent SGD steps. Set
$d=\alpha-\beta$. Immediately after the initial decision, the two branches
have errors $e_t^F=e$ and $e_t^D=e+h_0$, where
\begin{align}
    h_0&=-\eta_D d x+\eta_D x\nu_t,\\
    \mathbb E[h_0\mid e,x]&=-\eta_D d x, &
    \mathbb E[h_0h_0^\top\mid e,x]
    &=\eta_D^2(d^2+\sigma_D^2)xx^\top.
\end{align}

The conditional teacher moments are required at this fixed current
information. The vector $h_0$ is the parameter perturbation induced by
the initial query under the coupled counterfactual construction, using
the same teacher output as for the operational response.

For subsequent learning, couple the branches using the same samples,
targets, and update rules. Use $e_0^F=e$, $e_0^D=e+h_0$ and an
\emph{update index} $j$, distinct from the operational round index $t$.
For a branch $u\in\{F,D\}$ and $j\geq1$,
\begin{equation}
    e_j^u=A_j e_{j-1}^u+\xi_j,\qquad
    A_j=I-\eta X_jX_j^\top,\qquad
    \xi_j=\eta X_j\epsilon_j.
    \label{eq:common-sgd}
\end{equation}
This is squared-loss SGD with target $(w^*)^\top X_j+\epsilon_j$.
For the base expected-value result, assume, conditionally on the current
information, that the pairs $(X_j,\epsilon_j)$ are i.i.d., independent of
$\nu_t$, with input law $P_X$ and $\mathbb E[\epsilon_j\mid X_j]=0$.
Assume $\mathbb E\|X\|^4<\infty$ and
$\mathbb E[\|X\|^2\epsilon^2]<\infty$, in addition to the teacher moments.
These sufficient moment conditions make all finite-horizon risks and
expectations below finite. Independence across pairs ensures conditional
centering even given the entire future input sequence; i.i.d. inputs alone
and centering only on each individual input would not ensure this.

Subtracting~\eqref{eq:common-sgd} gives, pathwise,
\begin{equation}
    h_j=A_jh_{j-1},\qquad h_k=P_kh_0,\qquad
    P_k=A_kA_{k-1}\cdots A_1,\qquad P_0=I.
    \label{eq:transport-path}
\end{equation}
The common target cancels from this causal difference, although it still
affects each trajectory. Since $e_k^D=e_k^F+h_k$, the exact risk identity is
\begin{align}
    R(e_k^F)-R(e_k^D)
    &=-2h_k^\top Me_k^F-h_k^\top Mh_k\notag\\
    &=-2h_0^\top P_k^\top Me_k^F
      -h_0^\top P_k^\top MP_kh_0.
    \label{eq:pathwise-risk-gap}
\end{align}
This pathwise identity alone is not a closed expression in expectation.

\paragraph{Risk transport operator.}
Define
\begin{equation}
    K_k=\mathbb E[P_k^\top MP_k],\qquad K_0=M,\qquad
    \mathcal T(Q)=\mathbb E[(I-\eta XX^\top)^\top Q(I-\eta XX^\top)].
\end{equation}
For i.i.d. future inputs with constant step $\eta>0$,
\begin{equation}
    K_{k+1}=\mathcal T(K_k),\qquad K_k=\mathcal T^k(M).
    \label{eq:transport-operator}
\end{equation}
Indeed, condition on $A_1$ in
$P_{k+1}=(A_{k+1}\cdots A_2)A_1$: the inner expected risk matrix is
$K_k$ by independence and identical distribution. This argument does not
commute the random matrices. Symmetry of $I-\eta XX^\top$ gives
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal T(Q)=Q-\eta(MQ+QM)
       +\eta^2\mathbb E[XX^\top QXX^\top].
    \label{eq:transport-fourth-moment}
\end{equation}
Thus $\mathcal T$ acts on risk matrices and preserves positive
semidefiniteness. Transport generally depends on fourth moments, not only
on $M$. No novelty within SGD theory is asserted for this operator.

\begin{theorem}[Transported adaptation value]
Under the conditional coupling, independence, centering, and finite-moment
assumptions above, for every finite $k\geq0$,
\begin{align}
    \Delta_k
    &:=\mathbb E[R(e_k^F)-R(e_k^D)\mid e_t=e,x_t=x]\notag\\
    &=2\eta_D d\,x^\top K_ke
      -\eta_D^2(d^2+\sigma_D^2)x^\top K_kx.
    \label{eq:transported-adaptation}
\end{align}
In particular, $\Delta_0=\Delta_R$ with the initial step set to $\eta_D$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Unroll the unqueried trajectory as
\begin{equation}
    e_k^F=P_ke+q_k,\qquad
    q_k=\sum_{i=1}^k A_k\cdots A_{i+1}\xi_i,
\end{equation}
Here empty products equal $I$, and $q_0=0$. By the i.i.d. pair assumption
and centering,
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb E[q_k\mid X_1,\ldots,X_k]=0,
    \qquad \mathbb E[P_k^\top Mq_k]=0.
\end{equation}
Independence of $h_0$ from all future pairs then implies
\begin{align}
    \mathbb E[h_0^\top P_k^\top Me_k^F]
    &=\mathbb E[h_0]^\top K_ke
      +\mathbb E[h_0]^\top\mathbb E[P_k^\top Mq_k]
      =-\eta_D d\,x^\top K_ke,\\
    \mathbb E[h_0^\top P_k^\top MP_kh_0]
    &=\operatorname{tr}\!\left(K_k\mathbb E[h_0h_0^\top]\right)
      =\eta_D^2(d^2+\sigma_D^2)x^\top K_kx.
\end{align}
Substitution into~\eqref{eq:pathwise-risk-gap} proves the result.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Exact sign criterion.}
If $x^\top K_kx>0$, define
\begin{equation}
    s_k(x,e)=\frac{d\,x^\top K_ke}{x^\top K_kx}.
\end{equation}
Since $\eta_D>0$, equation~\eqref{eq:transported-adaptation} implies
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_k>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    s_k(x,e)>\frac{\eta_D}{2}(d^2+\sigma_D^2).
\end{equation}
The right side depends on the size and noise of the pseudo-update;
$K_k$ determines the effective risk geometry after $k$ later updates.
This ratio is only a sign criterion, not a continuation value. If
$x^\top K_kx=0$, positive semidefiniteness implies $K_kx=0$, so
$\Delta_k=0$ and the ratio is undefined.

\begin{transportproposition}[Persistence under positive rescaling]
If, for a given $k$, $K_k=c_kM$ with $c_k>0$, then
$\Delta_k=c_k\Delta_0$ and
$\operatorname{sign}(\Delta_k)=\operatorname{sign}(\Delta_0)$.
\end{transportproposition}
\begin{proof}
Substitute $K_k=c_kM$ into both terms
of~\eqref{eq:transported-adaptation} and use $K_0=M$.
\end{proof}
If subsequent learning only positively rescales the original risk
geometry, it can change the magnitude but preserves the sign of the
effect. No converse is asserted; a zero scaling would erase the effect.

\paragraph{Accumulated adaptation and the two clocks.}
For $H\geq1$ evaluation stages, discounted from the first such stage, set
\begin{align}
    \overline K_H&=\sum_{k=0}^{H-1}\gamma^kK_k,\\
    \Delta_{\mathrm{adapt}}^{(H)}
    &:=\sum_{k=0}^{H-1}\gamma^k\Delta_k\notag\\
    &=2\eta_D d\,x^\top\overline K_He
       -\eta_D^2(d^2+\sigma_D^2)x^\top\overline K_Hx.
    \label{eq:accumulated-transport}
\end{align}
Here stage $k=0$ evaluates the post-query parameters before subsequent
learning, and stage $k$ follows $k$ common updates. These stages coincide
with responses $t+1,\ldots,t+H$ only if no common update intervenes before
$t+1$ and exactly one occurs between successive responses. Under that
schedule the current-round weight is
$\gamma\Delta_{\mathrm{adapt}}^{(H)}$, and $H=\tau$ covers the
$\tau$ responses preceding use of $Y_t$, whose last stage is $k=\tau-1$.
If pending feedback causes a different update schedule, use the actual
products before each response; the update index must not be identified
automatically with elapsed rounds. The i.i.d. assumptions must also hold
for that schedule before applying the expected-value theorem.

If $K_k=M$ at every evaluation stage, then
$\overline K_H=(\sum_{k=0}^{H-1}\gamma^k)M$. Multiplication by $\gamma$
recovers $B_H\Delta_R$, where $B_H=\sum_{r=1}^H\gamma^r$.
Thus the isolated horizon below is the device with no effective transport
or deformation, not the general learning dynamics.

\subsection{Counterexample: dynamic sign reversal with $M=I$}

This example concerns a fixed learner state and an explicitly specified
input distribution. Let $X=(G,R)^\top$, where $G\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$
and $R$ is an independent symmetric Rademacher variable. This Gaussian
component is specific to the example; no general Gaussian assumption is
introduced. The law is not rotationally invariant. Independence and
symmetry give $M=I$, and
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb E[\|X\|^2XX^\top]
    =\begin{pmatrix}
       \mathbb E[G^4]+\mathbb E[G^2R^2]&0\\
       0&\mathbb E[G^2R^2]+\mathbb E[R^4]
      \end{pmatrix}
    =\operatorname{diag}(4,2).
\end{equation}
For one common subsequent update with $\eta=0.4$,
\begin{equation}
    K_1=I-2\eta I+\eta^2\operatorname{diag}(4,2)
       =\operatorname{diag}(0.84,0.52).
\end{equation}
Take $x=(1,1)^\top$, $e=(-0.2012,0.2564)^\top$, $b=0$,
$\sigma_D^2=0.001$, and $\eta_D=0.2$. One admissible noise choice is
independent symmetric teacher noise taking values $\pm\sqrt{0.001}$,
with task and subsequent target noise zero. Direct calculation gives
\begin{align}
    \alpha=d&=0.0552, & \alpha^2&=0.00304704,\\
    \Delta_{\mathrm{now}}&=0.00204704, &
    \eta_D\|x\|^2&=0.4<1,\\
    \Delta_0&=0.0008950528, & x^\top K_1e&=-0.03568,\\
    x^\top K_1x&=1.36, & \Delta_1&=-0.001007973376.
\end{align}
In particular, the linear and subtracted quadratic contributions to
$\Delta_1$ are $-0.0007878144$ and $0.000220158976$, respectively.
Thus $\Delta_{\mathrm{now}}>0$, $\Delta_0>0$, and $\Delta_1<0$.

The teacher improves expected current task loss before consultation cost,
and its pseudo-update initially reduces population risk. After one common
subsequent update, the transported effect of that same pseudo-update on
population risk is negative. The initial update is not changed
retrospectively: it is the sign of its later counterfactual effect that
changes. Although $M=I$, $K_1$ is not proportional to $I$ because the
fourth-moment matrix is anisotropic. Second-order isotropy therefore does
not guarantee persistence of isotropy under learning, and single-update
isotropic compatibility is not a temporal sign guarantee.

\paragraph{Positive probability over inputs at the fixed state.}
For the fixed learner state and parameters above, the expected gains
$\Delta_{\mathrm{now}}$, $\Delta_0$, and $\Delta_1$ are continuous
functions of $x$. Their strict signs, together with the conservative
initial-step inequality, therefore persist in an open neighborhood of
$(1,1)^\top$. The intersection of this neighborhood with the branch
$R=+1$ contains an interval of $G$ around $1$, where the Gaussian density
is strictly positive. Consequently, conditional on this fixed learner
state $e$, the set of inputs for which the expected gains satisfy
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_{\mathrm{now}}>0,\qquad
    \Delta_0>0,\qquad
    \Delta_1<0
\end{equation}
has strictly positive $P_X$-probability. This statement concerns
probability over inputs, not over realizations of the teacher noise.
It does not establish that this learner state is reached with positive
probability by a natural SGD trajectory, that the phenomenon is frequent
or stationary, or that it occurs for every $e$ or every distribution
satisfying $M=I$. It only partially addresses the distributional
limitation of the earlier geometric existence result.

\subsection{Isolated-horizon routing comparison}

A purely myopic routing rule queries the costly predictor whenever
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_{\mathrm{now}}>C_D.
    \label{eq:myopic-rule}
\end{equation}

To expose the possible consequence of adaptation without yet solving the
full delayed-feedback problem, consider an idealized isolated horizon.
Suppose that the only future difference caused by the current routing
decision is a constant population-risk gap $\Delta_R$ over the next $H$
pre-feedback responses. Assume, in particular, no intervening updates and
no action-dependent differences in subsequent routing costs.

With discount factor $\gamma$, define
\begin{equation}
    B_H=\sum_{k=1}^{H}\gamma^k.
\end{equation}
Using the first-future-response discount origin
of~\eqref{eq:accumulated-transport}, its current-round contribution is
\begin{equation}
    \gamma\Delta_{\mathrm{adapt}}^{(H)}
    =B_H\Delta_R,
\end{equation}
and the corresponding future-aware comparison becomes
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_{\mathrm{now}}+B_H\Delta_R>C_D.
    \label{eq:isolated-rule}
\end{equation}

This criterion is an analytical device rather than a solution of the
delayed-feedback objective. In particular, it does not assume that the
first reliable correction necessarily erases the difference between the
two routing histories, nor that all effects disappear after feedback
arrives.

\paragraph{Isolated over-querying.}
Consider a configuration satisfying
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_{\mathrm{now}}>0,
    \qquad
    \Delta_R<0,
    \qquad
    B_H>0.
\end{equation}
For any nonnegative cost satisfying
\begin{equation}
    \max\{0,\Delta_{\mathrm{now}}+B_H\Delta_R\}
    <
    C_D
    <
    \Delta_{\mathrm{now}},
    \label{eq:over-querying-cost}
\end{equation}
the myopic rule~\eqref{eq:myopic-rule} queries the teacher whereas the
isolated-horizon rule~\eqref{eq:isolated-rule} does not. The interval in
\eqref{eq:over-querying-cost} is nonempty because
$\Delta_{\mathrm{now}}>0$ and $\Delta_R<0$.

This is an existence result for suitable routing costs under the isolated
comparison, not a statement about every cost or about the optimal sequential
policy.

Conversely, isolated under-querying occurs whenever
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_{\mathrm{now}}
    <
    C_D
    <
    \Delta_{\mathrm{now}}+B_H\Delta_R.
\end{equation}
This requires $\Delta_R>0$ and can already occur in the scalar model.
Under-querying and over-querying are therefore distinct mechanisms and are
not asserted here as a single general sequential theorem.

\subsection{Reliable feedback and remaining continuation problem}

Common coupling resolves a specified adaptation component, while the full
sequential problem allows the initial parameter change to alter later
routing decisions and updates. A common update rule alone does not enforce
common future routing or targets when decisions depend on the parameters.
Such action-dependent continuations require the full $Q_F-Q_D$ comparison.

For the minimal linear model, an ordinary reliable-target SGD update is
admissible when $Y_t$ arrives:
\begin{equation}
    \theta^+=\theta+\eta_Yx_t(Y_t-\theta^\top x_t),\qquad
    h^+=(I-\eta_Yx_tx_t^\top)h.
    \label{eq:reliable-transport}
\end{equation}
The second identity follows by applying the first rule to both histories
with the same $(x_t,Y_t)$ and subtracting. It does not require undoing the
pseudo-update. It transforms the accumulated historical difference rather
than necessarily erasing it. Along $x_t$ the multiplier is
$1-\eta_Y\|x_t\|^2$; orthogonal components remain unchanged. Contraction
in that direction requires $0<\eta_Y\|x_t\|^2<2$, and global erasure is
not automatic. Other $U_Y$ rules remain possible but are not needed for
this minimal choice. As specified in the protocol, $Y_t$ is revealed after
the response at $t+\tau$ and first affects a response at $t+\tau+1$.
This repeated current input, and feedback already pending in the current
state, need not satisfy the fresh i.i.d. assumptions of the transport
theorem. The pathwise identity still holds; the same expected-value
formula cannot be assumed without checking the conditional noise and
input assumptions for that sequence.

Starting from the same pre-query state, let $e_{t+k}^{F}$ and
$e_{t+k}^{D}$ denote the cheap-predictor errors under the two
counterfactual histories generated by the initial choices $F$ and $D$,
respectively. Intervening updates and delayed reliable feedback from earlier
samples are allowed.

A natural population-risk component of the accumulated adaptation value is
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_{\mathrm{adapt}}^{(\tau)}
    =
    \sum_{k=1}^{\tau}
    \gamma^{k-1}
    \mathbb E\!\left[
        (e_{t+k}^{F})^\top M e_{t+k}^{F}
        -
        (e_{t+k}^{D})^\top M e_{t+k}^{D}
    \right].
    \label{eq:counterfactual-adaptation}
\end{equation}
The discount origin in~\eqref{eq:counterfactual-adaptation} is the first
future response. Hence its contribution measured from the current round is
\begin{equation}
    \gamma\Delta_{\mathrm{adapt}}^{(\tau)}.
\end{equation}
Under the isolated assumptions with $H=\tau$, this reduces to
\begin{equation}
    \gamma\Delta_{\mathrm{adapt}}^{(\tau)}
    =
    B_H\Delta_R.
\end{equation}
For $\gamma=0$, future adaptation has zero weight in the current objective.

Equation~\eqref{eq:counterfactual-adaptation} uses operational round
indices. It equals~\eqref{eq:accumulated-transport} with $H=\tau$ under
the stage-to-response schedule specified there and the theorem's
conditional assumptions. In general, the schedule and continuation
policies still need to be specified. Moreover, the expression
captures only differences in the cheap predictor's population risk; the
complete continuation-value difference must also include future operational
responses and consultation costs.

\paragraph{Next open problem: structural persistence or reversal.}
The next objective is to check formally whether rotational invariance
with $M=cI$ implies $\mathcal T(c'I)=c''I$, and hence, by induction,
$K_k=c_kI$. This statement is not proved here. If it holds, the positivity
conditions needed to obtain a clean sign-persistence condition must be
examined. Only afterwards, if needed, should relevance be connected to
states induced by natural learning trajectories. No distributional model
for $e_t$, stationary SGD covariance, distribution shift, or experiments
are introduced. No regret bound or optimality claim for the complete
delayed-feedback policy is made.
