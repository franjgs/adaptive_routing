\section{Related work}

This is a provisional conceptual organization, not a completed literature
review or a claim of novelty. The consolidation checkpoints in Decisions 006--007 motivate the following
distinctions. Bibliographic coverage remains incomplete; the project
inventory distinguishes local papers from verified BibTeX entries.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Learning to Defer and model routing:} operational choice
    between predictors and the role of consultation cost.
  \item \textbf{Sequential Learning to Defer:} long-term consequences of
    current deferral decisions. Learning to Defer must not be characterized
    universally as myopic. Whether future value specifically arises from
    updating the autonomous predictor requires further comparison.
  \item \textbf{Active Learning and Expected Error Reduction:} supervision
    acquisition and expected future predictive error after updating the
    learner. These approaches are not limited to uncertainty sampling;
    cost-aware future error reduction is not itself a novelty claim.
  \item \textbf{Value of Information:} general valuation of information
    within decision problems. These frameworks may express the proposed
    problem; a tractable specialization is a research objective.
  \item \textbf{Active Knowledge Distillation / teacher querying:}
    teacher-query costs and student improvement, without assuming that
    such methods are necessarily offline or pool-based.
  \item \textbf{ThriftyDAgger (Hoque et al., CoRL 2021):} robot-gated
    human intervention both controls the current task and supplies
    demonstrations for policy updates. Novelty/risk and an intervention
    budget govern gating, without explicitly valuing the expected future
    learning benefit of the particular intervention in that decision.
  \item \textbf{TRACER (Rida, 2026 preprint):} deferred teacher calls
    serve current inputs and produce traces for continual surrogate
    retraining. This rules out broad novelty claims for learning from
    deferrals. Routing uses predicted teacher agreement/confidence and a
    parity constraint, without explicitly valuing the expected future
    surrogate improvement from the particular call.
  \item \textbf{Noisy-oracle active learning:} Sogawa et al.
    ~\cite{sogawa2013active} use pool-based expected-error-reduction with an
    asymptotic expected estimation-error criterion under a noisy oracle.
    Future learning value with noisy supervision is therefore not novel;
    the query does not replace an operational response on the current sample.
  \item \textbf{Selective sampling and interactive imitation learning:}
    Sekhari et al.\ \cite{sekhari2023selective}, published at NeurIPS 2023,
    combine noisy feedback, queried-sample online regression updates, and
    regret/query-complexity guarantees. SAGE predicts before querying;
    RAVIOLI executes the learner action and transitions before querying
    expert feedback. Margin/uncertainty governs querying, without an explicit
    prospective update-induced continuation-value term.
  \item \textbf{Cumulative mistakes versus queries:} Hanneke and Yang
    ~\cite{hanneke2021toward} study the optimal trade-off and information-seeking
    strategies including PickyActive and PickySplitting. The main formulation
    predicts before optionally querying a true label and is realizable.
    It does not replace the current prediction. Selective sampling cannot
    be characterized globally as myopic or as ignoring future learning.
  \item \textbf{Stochastic and locally competent teachers:} Dekel et al.
    ~\cite{dekel2012selective} study adaptive/adversarial instances, online
    updates, explicit query cost, and single stochastic or multiple locally
    competent teachers. Additional labels can be empirically detrimental;
    their reported significance is limited to few-teacher scenarios.
    Prediction precedes querying, which uses uncertainty/stability rather
    than an explicit update-induced continuation-value difference.
    Harmful supervision is not itself a novelty claim.
  \item \textbf{Abstention with limited feedback:} the previously reviewed
    Gangrade et al.\ \cite{gangrade2021online} receive labels only on
    abstention and compare cumulative mistakes and excess abstentions.
    This does not by itself establish an operational teacher response or an
    explicit value of a particular learner update in the routing decision.
  \item \textbf{Dual control and sequential decision making:} actions
    with current and future effects. Generic computational difficulty
    does not establish novelty; a simpler rule exploiting routing structure
    remains to be derived.
\end{itemize}

These neighboring literatures motivate a more specific question: whether
a routing decision before the final operational response can explicitly
account for both its immediate predictive consequence and the future
predictive/routing cost change of the cheap predictor induced by the same costly prediction
used to solve the current instance. Any distinction from existing work
remains subject to falsification. Computability, interpretability, and
guarantees are objectives, not established contributions.

Broad search is paused following the targeted audit. The delayed reliable
feedback protocol is now fixed; the linear single-update analysis does not
settle the evolving sequential continuation value. Gradient misalignment
and harmful updates themselves are not claimed as novel.
