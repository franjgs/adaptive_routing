\section{Related work}

This is a provisional conceptual organization, not a completed literature
review or a claim of novelty. The consolidation checkpoint in Decision 005 motivates the following
distinctions. Bibliographic coverage remains incomplete; the project
inventory distinguishes local papers from verified BibTeX entries.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Learning to Defer and model routing:} operational choice
    between predictors and the role of consultation cost.
  \item \textbf{Sequential Learning to Defer:} long-term consequences of
    current deferral decisions. Learning to Defer must not be characterized
    universally as myopic. Whether future value specifically arises from
    updating the autonomous predictor requires further comparison.
  \item \textbf{Active Learning and Expected Error Reduction:} supervision
    acquisition and expected future predictive error after updating the
    learner. These approaches are not limited to uncertainty sampling;
    cost-aware future error reduction is not itself a novelty claim.
  \item \textbf{Value of Information:} general valuation of information
    within decision problems. These frameworks may express the proposed
    problem; a tractable specialization is a research objective.
  \item \textbf{Active Knowledge Distillation / teacher querying:}
    teacher-query costs and student improvement, without assuming that
    such methods are necessarily offline or pool-based.
  \item \textbf{ThriftyDAgger (Hoque et al., CoRL 2021):} robot-gated
    human intervention both controls the current task and supplies
    demonstrations for policy updates. Novelty/risk and an intervention
    budget govern gating, without explicitly valuing the expected future
    learning benefit of the particular intervention in that decision.
  \item \textbf{TRACER (Rida, 2026 preprint):} deferred teacher calls
    serve current inputs and produce traces for continual surrogate
    retraining. This rules out broad novelty claims for learning from
    deferrals. Routing uses predicted teacher agreement/confidence and a
    parity constraint, without explicitly valuing the expected future
    surrogate improvement from the particular call.
  \item \textbf{Online active learning, selective sampling, and abstention:}
    the highest-priority unresolved threat to novelty. Querying may both
    avoid current prediction loss and supply a label for learner updates.
    Mathematical equivalence must be audited, including the feedback model
    and the treatment of cumulative prediction loss and query cost.
  \item \textbf{Dual control and sequential decision making:} actions
    with current and future effects. Generic computational difficulty
    does not establish novelty; a simpler rule exploiting routing structure
    remains to be derived.
\end{itemize}

These neighboring literatures motivate a more specific question: whether
an operational routing decision can explicitly account for the future
predictive/routing cost change of the cheap predictor induced by the same costly prediction
used to solve the current instance. Any distinction from existing work
remains subject to falsification. Computability, interpretability, and
guarantees are objectives, not established contributions.
