\section{Problem formulation}

\subsection{Operational replacement and delayed reliable feedback}

At round $t$, the system observes $x_t$ and routes before issuing its final
operational response. The cheap adaptive predictor is $F_{\theta_t}$ and the
costly, potentially fallible predictor is $D$. Write $A_t\in\{F,D\}$ for
the routing action, reserving $a$ for the local step-size quantity used in
the linear analysis. The operational response is
\begin{equation}
    \widehat y_t=
    \begin{cases}
        F_{\theta_t}(x_t), & A_t=F,\\
        Z_t=D(x_t), & A_t=D.
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}

When $D$ is selected, its output replaces the cheap prediction
operationally. The same realized output $Z_t$ also supplies immediate
pseudo-supervision for $F$ after the response, through
\begin{equation}
    S_t^+=U_D(S_t,x_t,Z_t),
    \qquad A_t=D.
\end{equation}
Thus, operational replacement and pseudo-supervision are induced by the
same teacher call; $Z_t$ is not a second, independently sampled teacher
response. Choosing $F$ does not generate this teacher-induced update.

The reliable target $Y_t$ arrives after a fixed delay $\tau\geq1$, under
both routing actions. Its arrival is exogenous and independent of routing:
querying $D$ neither purchases $Y_t$ nor accelerates its arrival. By
convention, feedback arriving in round $t+\tau$ is revealed only after the
operational decision and response of that round. Hence $Y_t$ can first
affect the prediction at round $t+\tau+1$, and the $\tau$ responses indexed
by $t+1,\ldots,t+\tau$ precede its use. Reliable targets associated with
earlier rounds may nevertheless arrive during this interval.

Once available, $Y_t$ may be used to correct or update $F$ through a
reliable-feedback update $U_Y$. This correction need not undo the earlier
pseudo-update exactly or merge the two routing histories. Its specific form
and its interaction with intervening updates remain part of the sequential
problem.

The state
\begin{equation}
    S_t=(\theta_t,\mathcal I_t)
\end{equation}
contains the cheap predictor parameters and the information available before
routing, including any relevant history, pending feedback, and timing
information. No finite-dimensional sufficient state is assumed at this
stage.

The base protocol assumes point-valued teacher outputs rather than richer
confidence information. In classification these correspond to hard labels;
the linear regression specialization developed below uses scalar responses
as pseudo-targets. Logits, confidence signals, multiple teachers, and
occasional or missing reliable feedback are left as extensions.

\subsection{Three distinct evaluation objects}

Three evaluation objects must be distinguished. Agreement with $D$ measures
imitation of the teacher. Task performance is measured against the reliable
target $Y$ and need not improve when imitation improves. Finally, the total
system objective combines task loss with the cost of routing to $D$.

Without observing $Y$, task performance with respect to $Y$ is not generally
identifiable from teacher outputs alone without additional assumptions on
$D$. Delayed reliable feedback provides task-level evaluation and eventual
correction without making the teacher query an active acquisition of ground
truth. Here, reliable feedback means that the realized target is eventually
observed; it does not imply absence of intrinsic task noise.

For a horizon $T$, discount factor $0\leq\gamma\leq1$, predictive loss
$\ell$, and incremental consultation cost $C_D(S_t,x_t)\geq0$, a routing
policy $\pi$ seeks to minimize
\begin{equation}
    J(\pi)
    =
    \mathbb E_\pi\!\left[
        \sum_{t=1}^{T}\gamma^{t-1}
        \left\{
            \ell(\widehat y_t,Y_t)
            +
            \mathbf 1_{\{A_t=D\}}C_D(S_t,x_t)
        \right\}
    \right].
    \label{eq:sequential-objective}
\end{equation}
The loss is assigned to the round in which the response is issued even
though its target is observed only later.

A query to $D$ therefore has two conceptually distinct consequences: it
provides an alternative costly response for the current input and supplies
early, imperfect supervision for the cheap predictor. Either consequence
may be beneficial or detrimental relative to choosing $F$.

\subsection{Immediate gain and sequential continuation}

Define the conditional risks of the two operational responses as
\begin{align}
    r_F(S_t,x_t)
    &=
    \mathbb E\!\left[
        \ell(F_{\theta_t}(x_t),Y_t)
        \mid S_t,x_t
    \right],
    \\
    r_D(S_t,x_t)
    &=
    \mathbb E\!\left[
        \ell(D(x_t),Y_t)
        \mid S_t,x_t
    \right].
\end{align}
Their immediate expected difference is
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_{\mathrm{now}}(S_t,x_t)
    =
    r_F(S_t,x_t)-r_D(S_t,x_t).
    \label{eq:general-immediate-gain}
\end{equation}
Thus $\Delta_{\mathrm{now}}>0$ means that routing to $D$ improves the
expected current task loss before consultation cost is taken into account;
no such superiority is assumed a priori.

For a fully specified state evolution, let $V^*(S)$ denote the optimal
expected remaining discounted loss and routing cost from state $S$, with
zero continuation beyond the horizon. The two action values can then be
written formally as
\begin{align}
    Q_F(S_t,x_t)
    &=
    r_F(S_t,x_t)
    +
    \gamma
    \mathbb E\!\left[
        V^*(S_{t+1})
        \mid S_t,x_t,A_t=F
    \right],
    \\
    Q_D(S_t,x_t)
    &=
    r_D(S_t,x_t)
    +
    C_D(S_t,x_t)
    +
    \gamma
    \mathbb E\!\left[
        V^*(S_{t+1})
        \mid S_t,x_t,A_t=D
    \right].
\end{align}
The optimal comparison is therefore
\begin{equation}
    Q_D(S_t,x_t)<Q_F(S_t,x_t),
\end{equation}
with no preference imposed in case of equality. The continuation terms
include the effects of pending reliable feedback, subsequent updates,
future operational responses, and later routing costs. At this level the
comparison is a formal characterization, not yet a computable routing
policy.

To avoid conflating different notions of future value, we reserve
$\Delta_R$ for the population-risk change induced by a single
teacher-driven pseudo-update. We use $\Delta_{\mathrm{adapt}}$ for an
accumulated adaptation quantity over an explicitly specified horizon and
discount origin. Neither quantity is, in general, identical to the full
continuation-value difference appearing in $Q_F-Q_D$, which may additionally
depend on future routing decisions, operational responses, and consultation
costs.

The next section derives $\Delta_R$ exactly for a minimal linear model,
identifies conditions under which its sign agrees or conflicts with
$\Delta_{\mathrm{now}}$, and formulates the remaining counterfactual problem
created by delayed reliable feedback.
